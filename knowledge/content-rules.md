# Content rules

## Rights

Petit Meme does not own the videos it lists. It presents them for discovery and entertainment, and says so in its terms of use. People may reuse what they download, resale included, because Petit Meme holds no rights it could restrict.

## Takedown

A rights holder asks for removal by email, with the DMCA procedure on the legal pages. For a valid request, the Creator archives the Meme. It leaves the site at once and stays in the administration. A valid counter-notification can bring it back. Accounts behind repeated infringement can be closed.

## What stays off the site

Only Published Memes are public. A Pending or Archived Meme shows nowhere: not on the site, not in search, not to Google.

## Protecting the files

The video files are the product. Two things pull against each other:

- **Google has to see the Memes.** Google video search brings people in, so every Meme page stays indexable, with its thumbnail and its video declared to Google.
- **Nobody should drain the library.** People run automated browsers to fetch every video and every sound. Each one costs bandwidth, and copies the library wholesale.

The rule that settles it: protect the export and the stream, never the page. A Visitor never meets a challenge before the video, because the shortest path to the video comes first. Instead:

- Playback links are signed and expire.
- Only Petit Meme's own server reaches the original files.
- A free export never serves the original, only the watermarked copy.
- Thumbnails and animated previews stay public, so Google can index them.
- Media is served from Petit Meme's own domain, so its links survive a change of provider.

## Embedding

Other sites can embed a Meme through Petit Meme's own player page. It plays the video full frame with a link back to Petit Meme, shows no consent banner, and refuses unpublished Memes.
