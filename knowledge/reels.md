# Reels

The phone feed that chains Memes one after the other. Reels is built on the model of Instagram Reels and X's video feed, not on the rest of the site: where the two disagree, the feed people already know wins.

## Where it lives

Reels is a phone and tablet screen, reached from the tab bar. On a computer it stays reachable by its address, and nothing points to it.

## How it should feel

- **The video is the screen.** The title sits over the video, and the actions sit in a rail of icons on the side, without labels, as in the feeds people already use. Screen readers still hear each action's name.
- **One swipe, one Meme.** A swipe never skips a Meme, and a fast swipe never lands on a black screen: the picture of the coming Memes is already there.
- **A Meme starts once the swipe has settled**, from its beginning, and loops at its end. A swipe let go halfway leaves the current Meme playing.
- **Sound plays when the browser allows it.** When it does not, one tap brings it back.
- **The way out is always there.** The tab bar stays on screen, and the title opens the Meme's page. Coming back picks up where the person left, and the next visit starts after the last Meme seen.
- **Pulling down on the first Meme refreshes the feed.**
- **Choices open as sheets.** Sharing or downloading asks for the video or the sound in a sheet that rises from the bottom of the screen, as in the phone's own apps.

## The phone's bytes

Only the Meme on screen plays. The next one starts loading once the current one is safe, and nothing loads ahead when the phone saves data. The quality follows the size the video really takes on screen, so a phone never downloads more picture than it shows.
